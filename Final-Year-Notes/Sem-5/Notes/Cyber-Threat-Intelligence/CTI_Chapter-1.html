What is Threat Intelligence?

Definition

Threat Intelligence is threat information that has been aggregated, transformed, analyzed, interpreted, or enriched to provide the necessary context for decision-making processes.

Threat Information Types

A cyber threat is any circumstance or event with the potential to adversely impact organizational operations, assets, or individuals. The individuals and groups posing these threats are known as Threat Actors.

Threat Information is any info related to a threat that helps an organization protect itself.

Major Types of Threat Information

  1. Indicators: Technical artifacts (IPs, hashes) suggesting an attack.
  2. TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures): Descriptions of actor behavior.
  3. Security Alerts: Human-readable notifications (advisories, bulletins).
  4. Threat Intelligence Reports: Prose documents describing TTPs, actors, and targets.
  5. Tool Configurations: Recommendations for setting up security tools (signatures, firewall rules).

Cross-Organizational Information Sharing

Many organizations share info internally (e.g., security team to sysadmins). However, Cross-Organizational Sharing is powerful.

Paradigm

“One organization’s detection to become another’s prevention.”

Benefits

  • Shared Situational Awareness: Leveraging collective knowledge.
  • Improved Security Posture: Better understanding of the threat environment.
  • Knowledge Maturation: Correlating unrelated observations.
  • Greater Defensive Agility: Adapting to changing TTPs rapidly.

1.6 Cornerstones of Threat Information Sharing

Successful sharing relies on four cornerstones:

  1. Legal Framework: Laws that permit sharing (e.g., GDPR exceptions for security).
  2. Technical Infrastructure: Tools to automate sharing (STIX/TAXII, MISP).
  3. Trust Relationships: Vetting participants to ensure data isn’t leaked.
  4. Privacy Protection: Mechanisms to sanitize PII before sharing.

Establishing Sharing Capabilities

When launching a sharing capability, follow these steps:

flowchart LR
    A[Goals & Objectives] --> B[Identify Sources]
    B --> C[Define Scope]
    C --> D[Establish Rules]
    D --> E[Join Community]
    E --> F[Ongoing Support]
  1. Define Goals: Align with business processes and security policies.
  2. Identify Internal Sources: Inventory sensors, tools, and data feeds.
    • Network Data: Router/Firewall logs, PCAP.
    • Host Data: OS logs, Registry, File attributes.
  3. Define Scope: What to share, with whom, and when.
  4. Establish Rules:
    • Redaction/Sanitization requirements.
    • Attribution policies.
    • Traffic Light Protocol (TLP) for handling designations.
  5. Join a Sharing Community: Industry-specific, regional, or global groups.
  6. Provide Support: Infrastructure, personnel, and funding.

Information Sensitivity & Privacy

Privacy Risk

Disclosure of PII (Personally Identifiable Information) is a key challenge. Policies must be in place to sanitize data before sharing.

Traffic Light Protocol (TLP)

Used to designate handling requirements:

  • RED: Not for disclosure, restricted to participants only.
  • AMBER: Limited disclosure, restricted to participants’ organizations.
  • GREEN: Limited disclosure, restricted to the community.
  • WHITE: Disclosure is not limited.

Participating in Sharing Relationships

Activities include:

  • Engage in Communication: Email lists, portals, conferences.
  • Consume Security Alerts: Verify source, assess impact (CVSS), and mitigate.
  • Consume Indicators: Validate, Decrypt, Decompress, Extract, Prioritize, Categorize.
  • Organize & Store: Use a knowledgebase for CVE, CPE, CWE, actors, and TTPs.
  • Produce & Publish: Enrich indicators with context, use standard formats (STIX/TAXII), and protect sensitive data.

Nation-states often act as central hubs (e.g., National Cybersecurity Centers) to coordinate sharing, provide vetting, and establish legal frameworks (e.g., NIS Directive, CISA).

Enrichment: Fact Checks & Extra Info (2025 Trends)

2025 CTI Trends

  • AI-Driven Threats: Adversaries are using AI for sophisticated phishing and deepfakes.
  • Ransomware Evolution: Shift to “double extortion” and RaaS (Ransomware-as-a-Service).
  • Supply Chain Attacks: Increasing focus on third-party vendors.
  • Predictive Intelligence: Moving from reactive to proactive threat hunting using AI/ML.
  • Integration: CTI is merging with Digital Risk Protection (DRP) and External Attack Surface Management (EASM).
